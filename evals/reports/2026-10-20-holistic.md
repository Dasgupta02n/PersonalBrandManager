# Can a well-prompted Claude subscription do what our tool does?

20 October 2026 · every AI feature tested against a strong single prompt

## The answer

For about 60% of what matters to users, yes. A well-written prompt on the same model, given the
same facts and rules, matches our output on most features one at a time, and beats us on comment
starters. For the other 40%, no: our drafts are clearly better, our style notebook captures the
user's style far better and learns by itself, and a chat doesn't keep a record of someone's work.

The most important feature, the safety check, is matched on output: a strong prompt catches the
same risks. Our edge there is that the check runs on every draft, with the user's rules already
loaded, without the user having to remember. The research shows that matters: people overshare
when they control posting, and rarely stop to check.

### How the weights were set

From the research report on the people this is for:
- **Safety check and drafts, 25% each.** Fear of looking boastful and the risk of oversharing are
  the best-supported problems. Fear of being seen to use AI makes sounding like yourself essential.
- **Wins from a note, 12%.** These users are short of time; one-minute capture is the daily habit.
- **The record, 10%.** It is what makes the product worth renewing in weeks with no post: appraisal
  material, the weekly review and a year in wins.
- **How it reads, 8%.** The calibrator for boastful, too modest or generic.
- **Ideas and profile refresh, 6% each.** "What to post" was unproven; profile freshness matters most
  to independent experts, a secondary persona.
- **Style notebook 4%, revisions 2%, comment starters 2%.** Supporting features.

## Where our edge is, and where it isn't

**Real edge today**
1. **Drafts.** The biggest, proven win: 86%, up from 57% at the first test. The editor's pick lifted
   usefulness to the reader (16% to 46%) without losing the person's voice (89%).
2. **The safety check runs every time.** The same output as a careful prompt, but nobody has to
   remember to run it, paste their rules or paste their record.
3. **The record.** Builds itself from daily notes. The market report names it, with judgement about
   what is safe to say, as the asset general assistants lack.
4. **The style notebook.** Much better at describing how someone writes, and it learns quietly.

**No edge yet**
- Wins from notes, ideas, profile refresh, revisions and how it reads: a strong prompt does as well.
- Comment starters: a strong prompt does better.

This matches the market report's warning that the real competitor is "free ChatGPT with the safety
of silence", and its advice to sell the check and the private record rather than drafting alone.
The tests add one thing: our drafts are now a genuine edge too.

## How the product got here

| Feature | First test | Now | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drafts | 57% | **86%** | Voice-first prompt, credit by role, a usable point for the reader, then three drafts and an editor's pick |
| Safety check | 93% false alarms | **7%**, every risk caught | Mostly a fix to the test: clean posts' story details are now in the record |
| Wins from notes | 65% | 48% (63% with fact-checker) | Prompt rebalanced twice; a stricter version lost usefulness, a looser one lost faithfulness |
| Post ideas | 30% | 58% | Grounded prompt; sharper angles plus a fact-checker scored 50% |
| Revisions | 17% | 56% | Change only what is asked |
| How it reads | 100% | 100% (not re-run) | — |
| Profile refresh | not tested | 40% | — |
| Style notebook | not tested | 80% | — |
| Comment starters | not tested | 27% | — |

## Cost

- **Testing so far:** about $47 of OpenRouter credit across all runs.
- **Running the product:** a checked draft now costs about $0.18 (three drafts and the editor's pick).
  An engaged member's month is estimated at $5.55 against the $6.27 AI allowance in the $29 price;
  the 30% input, 30% marketing, 30% profit after tax split still holds.

## How much to trust this

- **Small samples.** 10 to 46 cases per feature. Results near 50% mean "no clear difference", not
  a precise score. Only drafts clear our pass mark with confidence.
- **Synthetic test cases.** Written for this purpose with fictional people; experts should review a
  sample, especially the risky posts and the "how it reads" labels.
- **An AI grader.** Blind, run in both orders, but the same model family. The human review page
  (`human-review.html` in each run) is the check; two or three senior professionals should rate a
  sample.
- **API, not the chat app.** The tests call Claude Opus 5.5 directly. A Claude subscription's chat
  may behave a little differently.

## What to do next, in order of weight

1. **Pitch the edge we have:** "Nothing you'll regret posting", the check that runs every time, the
   private record that builds itself, and drafts that sound like you. Don't sell ideas or comments
   as differentiators.
2. **Comment starters:** adopt the strong prompt's approach (stay on the post, never give the user
   experiences they haven't had, prompt for what only they know). Cheap to fix and re-test.
3. **Profile and CV refresh:** add an editor pass for one consistent story and a paste-ready
   headline, like the draft editor's pick.
4. **Wins from notes:** decide whether to switch on the fact-checker (better on facts, costs about
   $0.57 a month per engaged member, but not yet past the pass mark).
5. **Post ideas:** find sharper angles that stay grounded.
6. **Make the tests harder and confirm the wins:** harder "how it reads" and safety cases, expert
   review of the test sets, human ratings of the review page, and drafts and the style notebook
   re-run on 30 or more cases.
